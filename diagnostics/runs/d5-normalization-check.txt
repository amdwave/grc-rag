D5 normalization, checked against FOUR independent fetches
==========================================================
Recorded 2026-08-19 during the M13 migration, from scratch files that
were deleted immediately afterwards. This note exists so the evidence
survives the bytes.

D5 says: EUR-Lex stamps a per-response Dynatrace RUM id into one
<script data-dtconfig="..."> attribute, so two fetches of the same
unchanged document differ in sha256 and agree in sha256_normalized.
The entry demonstrates it with a pair. The scratch directory turned out
to hold two further fetches of the SAME consolidated document, made
during M2 exploration, which makes the demonstration a four-way one.

Document: 02024R1689-20260727 (AI Act, consolidated), legal-content route.
Rule applied: strip /\sdata-dtconfig="[^"]*"/ then SHA-256.

  fetch                          raw sha256        normalized sha256
  committed corpus/raw/...html   dcf7d6c5ba9f6619  32df6567c98a006f
  scratch aiact-cons.html        161866cae8be287f  32df6567c98a006f
  scratch cmp.html               4fd66ae8cde190b2  32df6567c98a006f
  manifest-only (d5-fetch-pair)  a2ba185c2d01f1a9  32df6567c98a006f

Four raw hashes, one normalized hash. The rule holds.

Two details worth keeping:

* cmp.html was 853,873 bytes - byte-for-byte the length recorded in
  d5-fetch-pair.manifest.json, with a different hash. Same length,
  different content is exactly what a fixed-width telemetry id
  produces, and it is why a length check would not have caught this and
  a hash does.
* The raw bytes behind d5-fetch-pair.manifest.json were never kept;
  its row above is the hash pair the manifest records, not a
  recomputation.

Why the sources were deleted rather than committed: 3.6 MB of
near-duplicate fetches of two documents already committed under
corpus/raw/, distinguished only by a per-response telemetry id. The
accompanying *.headers files were deleted and NOT committed for a
different reason - they carry live ELX_SESSIONID and AWS load-balancer
session cookies, which do not belong in a public repo.
